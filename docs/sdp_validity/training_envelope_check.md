# Training envelope check: do the PACE spectra fed to SDP sit inside what the model was trained on

Written 2026-10-13. Experiment `gulf_stream_20240305_20260531`. Everything below is measured from files on disk unless a bullet sits under "Inferences".

How to read the sources. Every script and log named below is in `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-jerry-school-research-eddy-tracking/f0bf5965-0ad9-48b3-9229-c252a9f4a9fc/scratchpad/envelope/`. That directory belongs to one session and may be cleared, so the scripts are short and rerun from the repo root with `uv run python <script>` in the order 03a, 04 (arguments `500 50 2000 main`), 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11. Nothing was written into either repository or into `data/` except this note.

Terms used throughout:
- Scored sample: 11,957 pixels with `inside_contour` true, drawn from `silver/collocate_pace/<polarity>/eddy_<id>_rrs.parquet` for composites that are rows of `gold/eddy_pigment_table.parquet` (3,571 composites, 2024-03-08 to 2025-12-29). The 24 strata are calendar month by side of the Gulf Stream. Up to 50 composites and 500 pixels per stratum, random seed 20261013 (`04_sample_run.py`). 394 of them have no SST or SSS at the pixel and the pipeline drops such pixels, so 11,563 pixels were scored. Stratum sizes run from 380 (January, north side, only 8 composites exist) to 500.
- Side: `N` when the gold `gs_dist_km` of the eddy center on that composite is positive, `S` otherwise. It is the side of the eddy center, not of each pixel.
- Weighted: pooled shares use the weight (interior pixels in the gold table for that stratum) / (sampled pixels in that stratum), so they estimate a share over all 1,249,453 interior pixels. By-stratum numbers are plain shares.
- Training spectra: the 145 rows of `sdp-validation/experiments/kramer_hplc_clustering/inputs/in_situ_hplc/Kramer-etal_2021_global_PANGAEA-937536.tab` (Rrs at 400 to 700 nm in 1 nm steps, temperature, salinity, HPLC pigments), pushed through the pipeline's own `get_rrs_residuals` and second difference (`03a_train_inversion.py`).
- LOO: leave-one-out. Each training spectrum is scored against a standardization and a principal component fit made from the other 144, which is the fair comparison for a PACE pixel the model never saw.
- D2: squared Mahalanobis distance in score space, the sum over the first K components of (score squared / training variance of that component). K = 20 is the main case because the training script keeps a median of 14 to 23 components per pigment; K = 10 and K = 30 are given as checks.

### Inferences
- The envelope fails first where the sun is low and the water is cold and rich in colored dissolved matter (north side, November to March). That combination is nearly absent from the training set: 2 of 145 samples are from north of 25 N in October to February.
- For most of the record the pixels are near the edge of the training cloud, not far beyond it: the first 10 components are inside, and the departure is a steady shift on weak components. Section "Roughness" traces that shift to a few wavelengths.
- SST only enters through pure-water backscatter, so the cold north-side pixels outside the SST range matter less than their D2 suggests.

### Gaps

- January on the north side rests on 8 composites (380 pixels), February on 14.
- Side is assigned per composite from the eddy center. A per-pixel side would need the daily axis distance grid.

## Reconstruction: how much of a PACE second-derivative spectrum lies outside the retained components

### Takeaway
A PACE spectrum carries about 9 times the out-of-span energy of a held-out training spectrum (84% of its standardized variance is outside the first 20 components, against 51%), concentrated at 401 to 480 nm with sharp peaks at 429 to 432 and 485 to 486 nm. This excess barely reaches the Tchla output, because the Tchla coefficients lie 97% inside those 20 components. Resampling the training spectra to OCI bands with the pipeline's preprocessing does not produce the excess, so it comes from the satellite spectra themselves.

### Inferences
- OCI band spacing, the 24 nm gap, and the 5 nm smoothing cost some skill (r2 down by about 0.1) but do not push spectra out of the envelope. The out-of-span energy in real PACE spectra therefore reflects sensor noise, atmospheric correction, or features of the sunlight spectrum that in-water radiometers do not record the same way.
- 430.8 nm and 486.1 nm are strong solar absorption lines (the G band and hydrogen beta). The peaks at 429 to 432 and 485 to 486 nm are most likely an imperfect cancellation of those lines in the satellite reflectance. This attribution is not tested here.
- A large out-of-span share is a sign that the spectra differ from the training kind, but for this model it is not the route by which outputs go wrong. The in-span shifts in the next section are.

### Gaps
- The in-span against out-of-span split was computed for Tchla only, with the mean of the 100 coefficient sets standing in for their median.
- The OCI bandpass was taken as a 5 nm Gaussian; the real response functions were not used.

### Inferences
- The integer labels are a real defect with a measured cost. The truncation moves every band toward the blue by half a nanometer on average, and the stored model responds with Tchla 1.4 times higher on clean in situ spectra as well as on PACE. Fixing the labels would bring the SDP level closer to the independent chlorophyll products (median ratio near 1.7 in place of 2.3, estimated by multiplying, not measured on composites) and would raise the share of zero-clipped Tchla pixels to about three times its present value. The present 7% zero share is therefore partly an accident of the labeling.
- A feature at 687 to 688 nm that peaks at the winter solstice and is stronger on the colder, more northern side behaves like a function of sun angle. 687 nm is the oxygen B absorption band, so the likely cause is a residual of that band in the atmospheric correction that grows with the path length through the air. Sun-induced chlorophyll fluorescence near 683 nm also varies with season and could contribute. Neither was tested.
- Because the training spectra have a quarter of their short-range variance at 2.8 to 4.5 nm periods and OCI cannot resolve it, the model's coefficients partly rest on structure PACE cannot supply. The control in the previous section puts the cost of that at about 0.1 in r2.

### Gaps
- The L3 `Rrs_unc` field is not in the collocated files, so noise could not be separated from fixed artifacts.
- Whether the true centers or the labels give pigments closer to the truth cannot be decided without in situ HPLC in the region; only the direction and size of the change are measured.

## Impossible outputs: where, when, and whether they follow the envelope

### Takeaway
Over all 1,249,453 interior pixels, 7.3% have Tchla clipped to zero, 8.4% have DV_chla above Tchla, and 15.6% fail at least one of three physical checks. Almost all of it is on the south side from September to January (Tchla zero in 41% of December pixels there); polarity does not matter. Being outside the envelope raises the odds of a zero by a factor of about 3, but 61% of zero pixels are inside it, and the stored model does the same thing to 10% of its own training spectra, so a zero is not a reliable sign of extrapolation.

### Cited Findings
- Full pass over `silver/pigments/<polarity>/*.parquet`, rows with `inside_contour` true, counted per composite one file at a time (largest file 18,658 rows in memory), restricted to the 3,571 gold composites; 5 composites with 21 pixels fall outside gold. Source for this whole block: `07_impossible.py`, `log_07.txt`, `impossible_by_composite.parquet`, `out_impossible_by_*.csv`.
- Thresholds. ### Inferences
- The impossible outputs are mainly a level problem, not an extrapolation problem. The model's errors are a few tenths of a ug/L in either direction even on its training set, so wherever true Tchla is near 0.1 a modest negative offset sends the prediction below zero. The south side from September to January is where a negative offset arrives (the seasonal 687 to 688 nm feature plus the rest of the red and green terms) while chlorophyll is still low.
- The envelope distance is a useful warning on the north side, where zeros are rare and nearly all of them are outside, and a weak one on the south side in autumn, where 22% of pixels inside the envelope are still zero.
- DV_chla clipped to zero in a quarter of summer pixels, mostly on the south side, is the opposite of what the biology predicts, since Prochlorococcus is most abundant in warm stratified Sargasso water. It points at the same seasonal offset acting with the other sign on the DV_chla coefficients, but that was not decomposed here.

- The band decomposition was done for Tchla only.
- The pixel files hold values after the clip. How far below zero the raw predictions go is available only for the scored sample (`raw_*` columns of `sample_scored.parquet`), not for the full record.

## Inversion health

### Takeaway
The inversion itself is healthy: none of 11,563 pixels failed to converge, and its chlorophyll is within a factor 2 of PACE `chlor_a` for 91% of pixels. The one failure pattern is on the north side in winter and spring, where for 1.2% of all pixels (20% in January on the north side) the fit drives chlorophyll to zero and assigns the absorption to CDM.

### Cited Findings
- The pixel files expose nothing about the inversion: they hold metadata and the 13 clipped pigments, and the L3 path drops a non-converged pixel after a print. All numbers here come from the rerun on the scored sample with `scipy.optimize.fmin` wrapped to record its output (`common.py`, `04_sample_run.py`, `06_roughness_iops.py`, `log_06.txt`).
- Convergence: `fmin` returned warning flag 0 for 11,563 of 11,563 pixels. Function evaluations: median 275, 99th percentile 672, maximum 1,220, against the limit of 2,000. The training spectra needed at most 501.
- Bounds: the search is an unbounded simplex, so nothing can sit on a bound. Negative CDM absorption 0.02% of pixels, negative backscatter 0.00% (5 of the 145 training spectra, 3.4%, have a small negative backscatter).
- Pixels dropped before the model for lack of ancillary data: 394 of 11,957 sampled interior pixels (3.3%), 393 of them for SST and 1 for SSS. By month: Jan 10.1%, Feb 10.8%, Mar 5.1%, Nov 3.8%, May 3.2%, Dec 2.5%, others 1.3% or less.

### Inferences
- Where colored dissolved matter is abundant, the three-parameter fit cannot tell its absorption from that of phytoplankton and gives everything to CDM. The residual spectrum then still contains the whole phytoplankton absorption shape, which the training residuals of comparable chlorophyll did not, and those pixels land outside the envelope.
- The SST gaps remove about a tenth of interior pixels in January and February before the model runs. That thins the winter composites that are already the least trustworthy.

### Gaps
- A fit can converge to a poor minimum without raising a flag; only the comparison with `chlor_a` catches that, and it shows about 1.7% of pixels a factor 10 off.
- The Level-2 path (`run_sdp_on_pace_l2`) was not exercised.
