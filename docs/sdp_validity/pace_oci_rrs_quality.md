## 1. Spectral sampling: what grid OCI Rrs is on, what is missing, and what interpolating to 1 nm and differencing twice does

### Takeaway
Between 400 and 700 nm the L2 and L3 AOP products carry 136 Rrs bands: 2.5 nm steps from 400 to 588 nm, no bands at all from 588 to 613 nm, 2.5 nm steps from 613 to 637 nm, and 1.25 nm steps from 640 to 700 nm, all with a 5 nm bandwidth, so neighbouring bands overlap and the true resolution is 5 nm everywhere. Interpolating to 1 nm adds no information; the 25 nm hole sits exactly on the steep water-absorption shoulder near 600 nm, and a second derivative there is invented by the interpolator.

### Cited Findings
- Read. OCI gives hyperspectral coverage from 340 to 895 nm with "5-nm bandwidth, with either 2.5- or 1.25-nm sampling, i.e., overlapping bands". The native CCD spectral sampling is 0.625 nm. In baseline science mode the on-board spectral aggregation is 2 or 4 CCD pixels depending on the tap, and "the L1B ground processing always aggregates to eight spectral pixels", so the bandwidth is the same whether the step is 1.25 or 2.5 nm. The region around 670 nm was given the finer 1.25 nm step to support fluorescence line height. Source: [Meister et al. 2024, IEEE TGRS 62, 5517418](https://pace.oceansciences.org/docs/MeisterIEEE2024AsPublished.pdf)
- Read. L1B band centers run 314.6 to 605.5 nm on the blue focal plane (119 bands) and 600.5 to 894.6 nm on the red focal plane (163 bands). From 590 to 610 nm both focal planes receive light through the dichroic and the bandwidth varies most there. The blue focal plane has an integrated out-of-band response of typically 2 to 3.5 percent above 340 nm, the red focal plane less than 1 percent, and the paper says the accuracy of the red focal plane data is superior because of lower stray light. Source: [Meister et al. 2024](https://pace.oceansciences.org/docs/MeisterIEEE2024AsPublished.pdf)
- Read. V3.0 (and later): "Rrs products in the 340-345nm and 590 to 610 nm spectral range have been removed, due to artifacts associated with instrument performance in those spectral ranges." The L1B known-issues list for V3 says data between 590 and 610 nm "often shows significant discontinuity ... and should not be used for science algorithms." Source: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes, release date April 2026](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf)
- Measured, V3.2 L2 (file above). `Rrs` and `Rrs_unc` have 172 bands, the header parameter is `wavelength_3d : 346:588,613:719`. Floating-point band centers: 400.18, 402.65, 405.13 ... 585.55, 588.09, then 612.73, 615.14 ... 637.30, then 639.79, 641.03, 642.26 ... 699.38. Counts inside 400 to 700 nm: 76 bands from 400 to 588 nm (median step 2.50 nm), 11 bands from 613 to 637 nm (step about 2.46 nm), 49 bands from 640 to 700 nm (median step 1.24 nm). Total 136. The integer wavelength labels in the file (400, 403, 405 ...) are rounded; the real centers are the floats in `sensor_band_parameters/fwave`.
- Measured, V3.2 L3m 8-day 4 km (file above). Same 172 wavelengths as L2. No band is added or interpolated by NASA in either level.
- Read. An OBPG forum thread of 8 October 2025 prints the same 172-value list from a V3_1 L2 file, so the band set did not change between V3.1 and V3.2. Source: [Earthdata Forum, "PACE OCI level-2 AOP wavelength_3d variable missing"](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?p=24325)
- Read (local PDF). Kramer et al. (2022) interpolated all in situ spectra to 1 nm and smoothed them with a 5 nm moving mean before any analysis. They repeated the analysis at 5 nm and 10 nm resolution: "very little loss of qualitative or quantitative power for pigment reconstruction between 1 nm and 5 nm resolution", and "at 10 nm resolution, the results are notably worse for all modeled pigments". They also say the model coefficients "have power across the visible spectrum", not only where pigments absorb. Source: [Kramer et al. 2022, RSE 270, 112879](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879), local copy `/Users/jerry/Zotero/storage/3S3D8JQG/`
- Read. NASA's own tutorial that runs the Python SDP code on an OCI L2 V3_2 granule logs an interpolated array with 374 columns, a residual array with 301 columns, and a second-derivative array with 299 columns. Source: [OB.DAAC notebook "Applying Spectral Derivative Pigments (SDP) ... to OCI data", last updated 24 July 2026](https://nasa.github.io/oceandata-notebooks/notebooks/oci/oci_sdp.html)
- Read. Vandermeulen et al. (2017) found, from variograms of first-derivative spectra over many water types, that "a continuous spectrum of 5 to 7 nm spectral resolution is optimal" and that finer sampling adds little. Source: [Vandermeulen et al. 2017, Optics Express 25, A785](https://usm.edu/ocean-science-engineering/_files/owx_pub_4_vandermeulen_etal_optics_espress.pdf)
- Read (local PDF). Cetinic et al. (2024) describe OCI as nominal 2.5 nm steps with about 5 nm bandwidths, with steps decreasing to 1.25 nm at the same bandwidth in part of the red. Source: [Cetinic et al. 2024, RSE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425723005163)

### Inferences
- Band overlap. An 8-pixel (5 nm) band stepped by 2.5 nm shares half its CCD pixels with each neighbour, and stepped by 1.25 nm shares three quarters. So instrument noise in adjacent bands is positively correlated (about 0.5 and 0.75 if the CCD pixels are independent), and the 1.25 nm red sampling does not give 1.25 nm resolution. This is arithmetic from the Meister numbers, not a statement I read.
- 374 = 719 minus 346 plus 1, and 301 = 700 minus 400 plus 1. So the NASA Python SDP interpolates the 172 bands onto every integer nm from 346 to 719, which means it interpolates straight across the 588 to 613 nm hole, then keeps 400 to 700. I did not read the code; this is inferred from the array shapes.
- What interpolation does to a second derivative. The 2.5 nm samples already come from a 5 nm boxcar-like bandpass, so the spectrum is band-limited; resampling to 1 nm cannot restore anything finer. The interpolator matters a great deal. Linear interpolation gives a second derivative that is zero between nodes and spikes at each node, which appears at 1 nm as a comb with the node spacing (a 2, 3, 2, 3 nm pattern, because 2.5 nm nodes fall on a 1 nm grid unevenly). A cubic spline or PCHIP gives a smooth derivative but its curvature between nodes is a property of the spline. The 5 nm moving mean that Kramer applied to the training data would suppress most of the comb if it is also applied to OCI. Whether Jerry's code does this is the code reviewer's question.
- Attenuation of real features. A 5 nm bandpass broadens a Gaussian feature of full width W to about sqrt(W^2 + 25) and scales its second-derivative amplitude by about (W^2 / (W^2 + 25))^1.5. For a 20 nm wide pigment feature that is a factor of about 0.91; for a 10 nm feature about 0.72. Kramer's training spectra were smoothed with a 5 nm moving mean, which has a similar effect, so the OCI bandpass by itself should not move OCI far from the training distribution. This agrees with Kramer's 5 nm test.
- The 588 to 613 nm hole. In clear Gulf Stream and Sargasso water (Measured, below) Rrs falls from about 6.5e-4 sr^-1 at 588 nm to about 2.5e-4 sr^-1 at 613 nm, a factor of 2.6 across the hole. That is the pure-water absorption shoulder near 600 to 605 nm, a strong real curvature feature that in situ 1 nm spectra resolve and OCI does not sample. After interpolation, about 24 of the 299 derivative points (8 percent of the predictor vector) carry no measurement, and the two points at the edges of the hole carry a curvature set by the interpolator. If any offset remains between the blue and red focal planes, it is also converted into slope and curvature across the hole. Because Kramer's coefficients have weight across the whole spectrum, these points are not harmless by default. Size of the effect on pigments: unknown, needs a test (zero those coefficients, or fill the hole with the fitted model, and compare).
- Kramer's 5 nm test is not a test of OCI. It degraded clean in situ spectra to 5 nm; it had no gap, no satellite noise, and no atmospheric-correction residuals.

### Gaps
- I did not find any published test of SDP, or of any second-derivative pigment method, on a grid with OCI's exact structure (2.5 nm, hole at 588 to 613 nm, 1.25 nm in the red).
- I did not confirm the spectral resolution of the in situ radiometers behind Kramer's training set. From memory, the Satlantic HyperOCR and HyperPro instruments have about 10 nm bandwidth and about 3.3 nm sampling, which would make the "1 nm" training spectra coarser than OCI before interpolation. Unverified; the method reviewer should check Chase et al. (2017) and the campaign papers.
- The NASA tutorial page does not state its interpolation scheme, smoothing, clipping, or flag handling.

## 2. Accuracy: validated OCI Rrs error by wavelength and version

### Takeaway
For V3.2 the only full validation is a NASA-team preprint (August 2026) that says deep-water Rrs meets the mission's baseline accuracy from 350 to 710 nm with a small negative bias in the green; no published work gives a band-by-band error spectrum at 2.5 nm, and none reports spectral structure of the bias fine enough to judge a second derivative. In clear water the per-pixel uncertainty NASA attaches to V3.2 Rrs is about 3 to 6 percent from 400 to 560 nm and 35 to 50 percent beyond 613 nm.

### Inferences
- The worst wavelengths in relative terms are everything beyond about 600 nm in clear water (signal about 2e-4 sr^-1 against an uncertainty of about 8e-5 sr^-1) and the deep blue below about 412 nm in absolute terms (largest unbiased RMSE and a slope of 1.09 to 1.16 in the project table).
- The project table is dominated by AERONET-OC sites, which are coastal. Its errors (unbiased RMSE near 1e-3 sr^-1 at 443 nm) are larger than the per-pixel `Rrs_unc` I measured in clear water (3.5e-4 sr^-1 at 442 nm). Both numbers describe total error, mostly broad in wavelength. Neither says how much error survives two differentiations.
- A bias that is smooth in wavelength (a slope of 1.04 at 443 nm, a negative offset in the green) mostly drops out of a second derivative of a model residual. What matters for SDP is error that changes sign within about 10 to 20 nm. No validation I found reports that.

- Chlorophyll was not available for the boxes I measured; "clear water" was chosen by blue-to-green ratio. The numbers apply to roughly the 0.05 to 0.3 mg m^-3 range but I did not confirm that with `chlor_a`.

## 3. Artifacts with spectral structure: which are documented, and which version fixed what

### Takeaway
NASA documents, and still lists as open in V3.2, residual water-vapour and oxygen absorption artifacts in the red (the O2 B band near 687 nm above all), blue-focal-plane crosstalk whose correction is deferred to V4, and erroneously high red reflectance at large view angles. These are narrow or sign-changing in wavelength, they vary with air mass and humidity, and they do not average away in a composite, so they are the main threat to a second derivative, mostly beyond 640 nm.

### Cited Findings
- Read. Gas absorption history. Source for all four: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf)
  - V2: a new gas transmittance correction that includes O2-O2 collision-induced absorption "resulted in reduced residual gas transmittance artifacts in the Rrs retrievals (e.g., near 360, 380, 477, 577, and 630nm)". V2 known issue: red Rrs "is likely to contain residual artifacts from water vapor and oxygen absorption near 680nm, 720nm, 760nm, and 820nm."
  - V3.0: "Significant improvement was made to the atmospheric gas transmittance algorithm and associated look-up table (LUT) that reduces residual error in the spectral vicinity of strong gas absorption features (H2O, O2, etc.). NO2 corrections were enabled." Rrs "generally increased in the blue spectral range, due to the instrument calibration and addition of NO2 corrections."
  - V3.1 (August 2025): "The absorbing gas corrections were further refined, leading to reduced artifacts near the O2b-band at 680-nm and spectral regions where water vapor absorption is significant."
  - V3.2 (April 2026): "The absorbing gas corrections were recomputed using the latest HITRAN database." Known issue retained: "Corrections for absorbing gases continue to improve, but Rrs variability, especially in the red, is still likely to contain residual artifacts from water vapor and oxygen absorption." nFLH "in extreme cases, is also impacted by the O2 b-band absorption artifacts in 680nm region."

### Inferences
- O2 B band. The 2 percent bump in the V3.2 vicarious gains at 685 to 692 nm is the calibration absorbing a residual oxygen-correction error at the HyperNAV sites. Water-leaving radiance near 687 nm in clear water is well under 1 percent of the top-of-atmosphere signal, so a 2 percent top-of-atmosphere adjustment is many times the ocean signal there. A fixed gain cancels the residual only for the air mass and surface pressure of the calibration sites; elsewhere a narrow residual remains and scales with path length. Evidence: the gain values (Measured) and NASA's own retained known issue (Read). Confidence that the 683 to 695 nm second derivative of OCI Rrs is not trustworthy in V3.2: high. Confidence about its size in pigment units: none, untested.
- The features at 430 to 432 nm and 485 to 487 nm are local maxima of Rrs that coincide with the solar Fraunhofer G band (430.8 nm) and hydrogen beta (486.1 nm). Candidates: Raman filling-in of solar lines in the water (real, and present in any radiometer with comparable bandwidth), or a residual of the solar-irradiance normalisation or spectral registration. I cannot tell which from one granule. Either way they are larger at OCI's 5 nm bandwidth than they would be in a 10 nm in situ radiometer, and their amplitude varies between scenes more than the rest of the blue. The features near 450 and 510 to 515 nm coincide with known weak shoulders of pure-water absorption (about 449 and 514 nm), so they are probably real and are what the SDP model residual is meant to remove. Confidence in the attributions: low to moderate.
- V3.2 fixed the cross-track BRDF error. BRDF and polarization errors are smooth in wavelength, so they change Rrs magnitude and the semi-analytical fit more than the derivative itself. They matter to SDP through the nonlinear fit and through any use of Rrs magnitude, not through narrow features.
- Blue-focal-plane crosstalk is uncorrected in every V3.x. NASA says it is worst in the UV; its size in the 400 to 590 nm Rrs is not published. Because crosstalk couples specific band pairs, it can leave a fixed fine-scale pattern. A fixed pattern would bias pigments by a constant and would not create false variability between eddies, unless it scales with scene brightness (near cloud).
- NO2 and ozone (Chappuis) absorption are broad compared with a 5 to 20 nm derivative window; NO2 has fine structure in the blue, but over the open Gulf Stream its column is small. I found no report of NO2 or ozone residuals in OCI Rrs after V3.0. Judged minor offshore; could matter near the US coast.

### Gaps
- No NASA document or paper quantifies the residual O2 B, water-vapour, or Fraunhofer-related structure in V3.2 Rrs in sr^-1. The release notes are qualitative.
- I did not find documentation of what `raman_opt = 2` changes in the distributed `Rrs` (as opposed to the Rrs passed to the IOP inversion). Unverified.
- I did not look for along-scan, mirror-side, or tap-boundary striping in V3.2 Rrs maps. The notes say mirror-side striping was "substantially reduced" in V2 and ghosting striping fixed in V3.1; no number for what remains.
- The water-vapour bands inside 400 to 700 nm (weak near 590 and 650 nm, strong from about 690 nm) are not individually discussed by NASA.

## 5. Signal to noise: pigment-driven second-derivative features against OCI noise and systematic error

### Takeaway
Random, band-independent noise is not the limiting factor in the blue and green: in a clear-water L2 pixel it is about 0.15 percent of Rrs near 442 nm, far below the thresholds in the derivative literature, though it reaches about 3 to 4 percent beyond 613 nm. The limit is that the real scene-to-scene variation of the second derivative in oligotrophic water is itself tiny (about 1 to 4e-6 sr^-1 nm^-2 on the 2.5 nm grid), comparable to single-pixel noise and to the documented systematic residuals, so at chlorophyll 0.05 to 0.3 mg m^-3 the method works at a signal-to-noise near one unless many pixels are averaged.

### Cited Findings
- Read. Vandermeulen et al. (2017): adding 3 percent Gaussian noise (signal-to-noise about 66) to normalised reflectance spectra was enough that 5 nm first-derivative data resolved no more than 10 nm data; with a 13 nm Savitzky-Golay smoother the tolerable noise rose to about 13 percent (signal-to-noise about 15). Source: [Vandermeulen et al. 2017, Optics Express 25, A785](https://usm.edu/ocean-science-engineering/_files/owx_pub_4_vandermeulen_etal_optics_espress.pdf)
- Read (local PDF). Kramer et al. (2022): "The derivative analysis approach magnifies narrow spectral features, including measurement noise and error"; "even small variations on short (5-10 nm) spectral scales are ultimately important in this pigment modeling approach"; "the noise-to-signal ratio across the visible spectrum for in situ Rrs data (as were used here) is much lower than for remotely sensed Rrs data. Thus, spatiotemporal aggregation of remotely-sensed Rrs will likely be required". They removed in situ spectra with high noise in the 610 to 660 nm range, and they expect atmospheric-correction errors "on broader spectral scales" that the approach would "negate". Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879)
- Read. Prochaska et al.: with a noise estimate taken from OCI V1.0 L2 `Rrs_unc`, only about 30 percent of a 3,320-spectrum synthetic data set favours a model that includes phytoplankton absorption over one without; hyperspectral data "may recover a 4th and possibly 5th parameter" describing absorption and backscatter. Their analysis assumes uncorrelated errors. Source: [Prochaska et al., arXiv:2408.06149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06149)
- Summary. Xi et al. (2015) found that separating taxonomic groups from derivative Rrs spectra was less accurate than from absorption spectra, and was partly restored only when strong water-absorption wavelengths were excluded and chlorophyll exceeded 1 mg m^-3; a later paper by the same group says pure-water absorption features in Rrs affect identification when phytoplankton concentration is low. Source: [Xi et al. 2015, Remote Sensing 7, 14781](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs71114781)
- Summary. A 2026 paper that reconstructs OCI-like spectra from MODIS warns that uncertainty in the residual reflectance between 413 and 490 nm calls for caution with "pigment retrieval algorithms that rely heavily on spectral derivatives or residuals". Source: [SA-CGAN paper, Journal of Remote Sensing](https://spj.science.org/doi/10.34133/remotesensing.1036)
- Read. OCI's instrument signal-to-noise exceeds its requirement at typical ocean radiance in every band, by at least 22 percent and typically about 80 percent. Source: [Meister et al. 2024](https://pace.oceansciences.org/docs/MeisterIEEE2024AsPublished.pdf)
- Measured, V3.2 L2, 600 clear-water boxes of 25 pixels. Second difference on the native grid, in sr^-1 nm^-2:
  - 400 to 588 nm (2.5 nm): median pixel-to-pixel standard deviation inside a box 5.3e-6 (about 1.0e-5 near 405 nm falling to 3.9e-6 near 583 nm); median between-box standard deviation of the box mean 1.5e-6 (range about 1.0e-6 to 3.6e-6); median magnitude of the grand mean 3.7e-6, with the fixed features listed in section 3 reaching 2e-5.
  - 613 to 637 nm (2.5 nm): pixel 3.7e-6, between-box 1.3e-6, grand mean 7e-7.
  - 640 to 700 nm (1.25 nm): pixel 7.7e-6, between-box 2.6e-6, grand mean 1.6e-6.
- Measured, same boxes. Pixel-to-pixel Rrs deviations are almost the same at neighbouring bands: correlation 0.97 to 0.99 with the adjacent band and 0.83 to 0.98 eight bands away. If the pixel scatter were independent between bands, the second-difference scatter would be about 7e-5 sr^-1 nm^-2; it is 13 times smaller.

### Inferences
- Size of the band-independent noise. Inverting the second-difference scatter (standard deviation equals root 6 times the per-band noise divided by the step squared) gives a spectrally independent noise per L2 pixel of about 1.4e-5 sr^-1 from 400 to 588 nm (0.15 percent of Rrs at 442 nm, about 1 percent at 555 nm), about 9e-6 sr^-1 from 613 to 637 nm (about 4 percent of Rrs) and about 5e-6 sr^-1 from 640 to 700 nm (about 3 percent of Rrs; this last figure is approximate because those bands overlap by three quarters). Against Vandermeulen's 3 percent unsmoothed threshold, single L2 pixels pass easily below 590 nm and sit at the threshold beyond 613 nm. Vandermeulen's thresholds are for first derivatives; a second derivative is more demanding, so "at the threshold" in the red should be read as failing without averaging or smoothing.
- Signal against noise in oligotrophic water. The between-box spread (1 to 4e-6) is an upper bound on what pigment differences contribute in this granule, since it also contains aerosol, geometry and artifact variability. Single-pixel noise (4 to 10e-6) is 2 to 5 times larger. So one L2 pixel cannot resolve pigment-driven derivative differences in clear water. An 8-day 4.6 km bin receives roughly 15 OCI pixels per clear overpass near nadir (4.6 km squared over 1.2 km squared) and often 1 to 3 clear overpasses, so 15 to 45 pixels, which lowers independent noise by a factor of 4 to 7, to about 0.8 to 1.4e-6. That is the same size as the signal spread. Rough signal-to-noise for the derivative in an 8-day bin in clear water: about 1 to 2 in the blue and green, lower in the red. This rests on one granule and on the assumption that pixel noise is independent between pixels, which is optimistic. Confidence: moderate for the order of magnitude, low for the exact ratio.
- This is consistent with the locally established factor-of-3 day-to-day swings in SDP Tchla at BATS: daily output at one site is noise-dominated.
- Systematic residuals do not average down. The O2 B and water-vapour residuals, the Fraunhofer-line features and any crosstalk pattern are the same sign across neighbouring pixels and days, and several of them have between-scene variability (3 to 6e-6 at 430, 450 and 665 to 690 nm) larger than the typical signal spread. After compositing, they are what is left. Whether they bias all eddies equally (harmless for contrasts) or vary with humidity, air mass and view angle (harmful) is untested; the gas residuals at least must vary with path length and water vapour.
- The red end carries less information than its band count suggests. 49 of the 136 bands lie between 640 and 700 nm, where Rrs is about 2e-4 sr^-1, total uncertainty is 35 to 50 percent, and the documented gas residuals live. In a regression on principal components of the second derivative, noisy red points can dominate the scores unless the training variance there was large.
- Kramer's expectation that atmospheric-correction error is spectrally broad holds for aerosol error and is supported by the 0.97 to 0.99 band-to-band correlation measured here. It does not hold for gas-correction error, which is narrow by nature.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that states the size of pigment-driven second-derivative features in Rrs at chlorophyll 0.05 to 0.3 mg m^-3. The method reviewer can get it from Kramer's training data: the spread of the second derivative among the low-chlorophyll spectra, in sr^-1 nm^-2, to compare with the noise figures above. The comparison must be made after the same interpolation and smoothing.
- Torrecilla et al. (2011), Uitz et al. (2015), Wolanin et al. (2016), Chase et al. (2017), Bracher et al. and Cael et al. (2020) were not read today; none surfaced with a noise threshold for second-derivative Rrs. Lee et al. was not checked.
- No paper using real OCI data reports the noise of a derivative spectrum.
- My measurement is one April granule. A summer granule with more water vapour, and one at a large view angle, would show how much the red and the 430 and 450 nm features move.

## 6. Compositing: how an L3 8-day 4 km bin is built and what it does to a nonlinear algorithm

### Takeaway
An L3 mapped Rrs value is a plain arithmetic mean of every unmasked L2 pixel that fell in a 4.6 km bin during the 8 days, with the same pixel set at every wavelength, and the mapped file carries neither the number of observations nor any uncertainty. Averaging commutes with the derivative but not with the model fit or with clipping at zero; standard guidance is to run nonlinear algorithms at L2 and then average, but for a noise-dominated retrieval with clipping, running on the composite is the less biased order, at the cost of mixing different days and water masses in one spectrum.

### Cited Findings
- Read. "Level-3 products are generally global daily, 8-day, or monthly composites ... The mapped products are derived by reprojection of binned data (Level-3b), where the typical bin distribution is a quasi-equal-area 4.6km or 9.2km integerized sinusoidal grid." Source: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf)
- Measured, V3.2 L3m file. Global attribute `measure = Mean`, `spatialResolution = 4.638312 km`, built by `l3mapmerge` (OCSSW V2026.2). Variables: `Rrs` (lat, lon, 172 wavelengths), `nflh`, `avw`, `aot_865`, `angstrom`. There is no `Rrs_unc`, no observation count, no scene count, no variance, and no flag variable. The local downloader drops only the `palette` variable, so this is the content NASA distributes in the mapped file.
- Read. Flags and masks, by version. Source: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf)
  - View zenith above 60 degrees: flagged at L2 and "masked in Level-3 products" since V2 (V1 flagged at 75 degrees).
  - Straylight: "Data within 1 pixel along track and within 2 pixels across track from a bright source such as a cloud are flagged ... These straylight flagged pixels are masked in Level-3 products", since V2.
  - Extreme sun glint at the tilt change: masked at L2 with no retrieval, since V3.0.
  - Coccolithophores: masked in all L3 ocean colour products through V3.1; "In V3.2, that masking has been disabled for the AOP products".
- Measured, V3.2 L2 header. L2 masks (no retrieval): `ATMFAIL, LAND, CLDICE, HILT`. `satzen = 60`, `maskglint = 0`, `masksatzen = 0` at L2, so high-view-angle and moderate-glint pixels have Rrs at L2 and are removed only at binning. Flag percentages in this granule: high view zenith 14.4, straylight 7.9, moderate glint 8.7, absorbing aerosol 4.5.
- Measured, V3.2 L2. Of 45,939 sampled pixels that pass an L3-like mask, none has a fill value at some wavelengths and data at others. Negative Rrs are kept as data.
- Read. IOCCG Report 4 on binned products: "Since most algorithms involve non-linear functions of the input fields, we would expect the two methods to differ. In general, one should evaluate a non-linear function at the highest spatial and temporal resolution possible, and then average the results to obtain its mean over time and space." It adds that computing from daily L2 introduces a clear-sky sampling bias, and that "Users should nevertheless be aware of the possible limitations when using Level-3 data in highly non-linear models." Source: [IOCCG Report 4 (2004)](https://www.ioccg.org/reports/report4.pdf)
- Read. NASA's own products follow that order: IOP and BGC products are computed per pixel at L2 and then binned. The exception is MOANA, an L4 product whose inputs are "level 3 remote sensing reflectance and SST". Sources: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf), [MOANA ATBD](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/atbd/atbd-obdaac-phytoplankton-groups-MOANA.pdf)
- Read. NASA's tutorial runs SDP on L2 granules; its companion tutorial runs the Gaussian pigment method (Chase et al. 2017) on "Level-2 or Level-3 Mapped AOP products". Sources: [SDP notebook](https://nasa.github.io/oceandata-notebooks/notebooks/oci/oci_sdp.html), [GPig notebook](https://nasa.github.io/oceandata-notebooks/notebooks/oci/oci_gpig.html)
- Read (local PDF). Kramer et al. (2022) expected that "spatiotemporal aggregation of remotely-sensed Rrs will likely be required" to reach a tolerable signal-to-noise. Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879)

### Inferences
- Each wavelength averages the same observations. The masks are per pixel, not per band, and no unmasked pixel in the granule had partial fill, so a bin's spectrum is the mean of whole spectra. Compositing therefore does not create band-to-band steps by itself. Confidence: high for V3.2, from one granule plus how the binner treats flags.
- Which steps commute with the mean. Interpolation, the 5 nm smoothing, the second derivative, the projection on principal components and the linear regression are all linear, so for those steps "average then compute" equals "compute then average". Three steps are not linear: the semi-analytical fit that produces the residual (and its inputs, since salinity and temperature would also be 8-day values), any log transform of the pigments, and clipping negative pigments to zero.
- Clipping. The mean of clipped values is always at least the clipped mean. If daily L2 pigments scatter by a factor of 3, as found locally at BATS, then clipping each day and averaging pushes low pigments up, and the bias grows as the true value approaches zero, which is exactly the minor pigments at chlorophyll 0.05 to 0.3 mg m^-3. Running SDP once on the 8-day mean spectrum clips once, after the noise has been averaged. For this error source the composite-first order is better. This contradicts the general IOCCG advice only because that advice assumes the per-pixel retrieval is not noise-dominated.
- The fit. A fit to a mean spectrum is not the mean of fits, but the fit targets broad spectral shape, where the day-to-day differences inside a bin are small relative to the signal. I expect this nonlinearity to matter less than clipping. Untested.
- Mixed water. A bin near an eddy edge that moves several km per day sees different water on different days, and cloud decides which days count. Two neighbouring bins can be built from different days, with different aerosol and gas-correction errors, so the composite can show bin-to-bin texture that is sampling, not ocean. The mean spectrum of two water types is a valid mixture for the linear steps, so SDP returns roughly the mean pigments of the mixture, not a wrong community; what is lost is the sharpness of the edge and the knowledge of which days contributed. The L3m file cannot tell a 1-pixel bin from a 60-pixel bin.
- Pixels with few observations are kept. I saw no minimum-count rule in the notes or the file; a bin with a single unmasked L2 pixel appears in the 8-day map with that pixel's noise (about 5 times the noise of a 25-pixel bin). Plausible but unverified for the OCI AOP suite.
- Scan-edge and cloud-edge protection is in place. Since V2, pixels above 60 degrees view zenith and pixels next to bright targets never reach L3, so the red scan-edge artifact and cloud straylight are mostly excluded from composites. Moderate glint and absorbing-aerosol pixels are probably not excluded (not confirmed).
- The V3.2 removal of coccolithophore masking lets bright, strongly scattering water into AOP composites. That is a regime outside Kramer's training set; it is relevant on the shelf and slope north of the Gulf Stream in late spring.

### Gaps
- The exact list of L2 flags that the binner masks for the OCI AOP suite in V3.2 could not be read: the l2_flags documentation page redirects and the full-size L3 file's `processing_control` group is not in the local subset. To settle it, read `processing_control/input_parameters/flaguse` in one full L3b or L3m file, or the OCI `l2bin_defaults.par` in OCSSW.
- Whether the L3 binned (L3b) AOP files carry observation counts, scene counts and sums of squares for Rrs, as OBPG binned files normally do, was not checked. If they do, they give a per-bin count and variance that the mapped files lack.
- I found no study that compares a derivative or pigment algorithm run on composited Rrs with the same algorithm run on L2 and then averaged, for OCI or any sensor. NASA's l2gen versus l3gen comparison for IOPs exists (an IOP workshop page) but its results could not be read today.
- I found no documentation of how the 8-day L3 handles a bin observed twice in one day by overlapping swaths at 40 N.

## 7. Uncertainty products: is there a per-pixel Rrs uncertainty that could be propagated?

### Takeaway
Yes at L2 and no at L3: L2 AOP carries `Rrs_unc` for all 172 bands, but only the per-band standard uncertainty, without the band-to-band covariance that a second derivative and a regression need. Treated as independent it overstates the random error of a second derivative by more than ten times; treated as fully correlated it would wrongly cancel.

### Cited Findings
- Measured, V3.2 L2. `geophysical_data/Rrs_unc`, "Uncertainty in remote sensing reflectance", same shape as `Rrs` (lines, pixels, 172), units sr^-1. No covariance variable exists in the file. Clear-water magnitudes are in section 2.
- Read. `Rrs_unc` is listed as a Provisional product of the OC_AOP suite in V2 and V3.x. Source: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf)
- Measured, V3.2 L3m. No `Rrs_unc` (section 6).
- Summary. The method is the derivative-based (analytical) error propagation of Zhang et al. (2022), developed for MODIS and NASA's multiple-scattering epsilon atmospheric correction, checked against Monte Carlo runs; the sources are instrument random noise, instrument systematic uncertainty, and forward-model uncertainty. Source: [Zhang et al. 2022, Optics Express 30, 31415](https://mdsoar.org/items/3ba1c844-fd54-4560-8474-a5b73dfe156d)
- Read. Zhang et al. (2025) state the problem directly: algorithms that use Rrs at several bands need the spectral error covariance; for N bands there are N(N+1)/2 elements per pixel; storing the full matrix would make a 5-minute OCI L2 file about 60 GB, and a polynomial fit to the covariance brings it to about 1.7 GB. They note the covariance "was often spectrally smooth". Source: [Zhang et al. 2025, Frontiers in Remote Sensing](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/remote-sensing/articles/10.3389/frsen.2025.1670390/full)
- Read (local PDF). Cetinic et al. (2024) note that PACE has a requirement to estimate uncertainty at the level of Rrs. Source: [Cetinic et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0034425723005163)
- Measured. In clear water `Rrs_unc` at 442 nm is 3.5e-4 sr^-1, while the band-independent noise implied by the pixel scatter of the second difference is about 1.4e-5 sr^-1 (section 5). If `Rrs_unc` were independent between bands the second difference would scatter by about 7.4e-5 sr^-1 nm^-2; the observed pixel scatter is 5.3e-6.

### Inferences
- `Rrs_unc` is dominated by components that are shared across neighbouring bands (aerosol selection, calibration, forward model). Those largely cancel in a second derivative. So `Rrs_unc` alone cannot be pushed through the SDP regression: the variance of a linear combination needs the full covariance, and the two limiting assumptions differ by more than an order of magnitude.
- A workable substitute for the random part is empirical: the scatter of SDP output among the L2 pixels inside one 4 km bin, or among neighbouring bins, measures the noise that actually reaches the pigments. It says nothing about the systematic part.
- The covariance work of Zhang et al. is the route NASA is taking; the V3.2 file checked here does not yet include it. If a later version adds fitted covariance coefficients, formal propagation through the linear part of SDP becomes possible.

### Gaps
- I did not read an OCI-specific description of how `Rrs_unc` is computed operationally in V3.2, or whether it includes the gas-correction and vicarious-gain uncertainty. The Zhang 2022 paper is MODIS-based.
- Whether `Rrs_unc` has been validated against matchups for OCI is unknown to me; the Ibrahim preprint may address it.

## 9. Issue triage for SDP on L3 8-day 4 km Rrs in the Gulf Stream region

### Takeaway
Four input-side issues are real: the unsampled 588 to 613 nm hole, residual gas and solar-line structure concentrated beyond 640 nm and at a few blue wavelengths, a signal-to-noise near one for derivative features in oligotrophic water, and the absence of counts and uncertainty in the L3 mapped file. Spectral resolution, band-to-band averaging mismatch, random instrument noise in the blue, and the current version mix are not problems.

### Cited Findings
- All evidence is cited in sections 1 to 8; the items below point back to it. Primary sources: [PACE OCI V3 Processing Notes](https://oceandata.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/files/reprocessing/PACE_OCI_V3_Release_Notes.pdf), [Meister et al. 2024](https://pace.oceansciences.org/docs/MeisterIEEE2024AsPublished.pdf), [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879), and the two local files named at the top.

### Inferences
Real issues, with evidence, size and confidence:

- The 588 to 613 nm hole (sections 1, 3).
  - Evidence: NASA removed 590 to 610 nm in V3.0; the file has no band between 588.09 and 612.73 nm; clear-water Rrs drops by a factor of 2.6 across it; NASA's SDP code interpolates across it.
  - Size: about 24 of 299 second-derivative points have no data, plus two edge points whose curvature is set by the interpolator. Effect on pigments unknown.
  - Confidence that the input is invalid there: high. Confidence that it changes pigments materially: unknown until the regression weights in that range are examined.
- Residual gas-absorption structure in the red, strongest at the O2 B band (sections 3, 5).
  - Evidence: listed as a known issue in every version including V3.2; vicarious gains carry a 2 percent bump at 685 to 692 nm; between-scene spread of the second difference peaks at 665 to 673 and 688 to 696 nm.
  - Size: between-scene spread up to 6e-6 sr^-1 nm^-2 where the typical value is 2.6e-6, in a region where Rrs is 2e-4 sr^-1 with 35 to 50 percent uncertainty.
  - Confidence: high that 680 to 700 nm derivative values in V3.2 are contaminated; moderate that 640 to 680 nm is degraded mainly by low signal.
- Narrow features at 430 to 432, about 450, and 485 to 487 nm whose amplitude varies between scenes (section 3).
  - Evidence: one granule. Positions match solar lines and water-absorption shoulders.
  - Size: mean features of 1 to 2.6e-5 sr^-1 nm^-2 with between-scene spread up to 3.6e-6, about twice the blue median.
  - Confidence: moderate that they exist in V3.2; low on cause; unknown whether in situ training spectra contain the same features at the same strength.
- Derivative signal-to-noise near one in oligotrophic water (section 5).
  - Evidence: single-pixel second-difference noise 4 to 10e-6 against a scene-to-scene spread of 1 to 4e-6; consistent with the factor-of-3 daily swings already seen at BATS and with Kramer's own warning.
  - Size: an 8-day bin with 15 to 45 pixels reaches a ratio of roughly 1 to 2; a bin with one pixel is about 5 times worse.
  - Confidence: moderate.
- No observation count or uncertainty in L3 mapped files (sections 6, 7).
  - Evidence: file contents.
  - Size: bins built from 1 pixel and from 60 pixels look the same; their noise differs by a factor of about 8.
  - Confidence: high. Mitigation exists (L3 binned files if they carry counts, or self-made composites from L2).
- Clipping bias when averaging per-day or per-pixel outputs (section 6).
  - Evidence: mathematics plus the local finding that daily output scatters by a factor of 3.
  - Size: grows as the true pigment approaches zero; not computed.
  - Confidence: high on direction (upward for minor pigments if clipped before averaging), unknown on size. Favors running SDP on the composite, or averaging unclipped L2 outputs and clipping last.
- Uncorrected blue-focal-plane crosstalk, 310 to 610 nm, deferred to V4 (section 3).
  - Evidence: NASA known issue, "significant in some bands, particularly in the UV".
  - Size in the visible: not published.
  - Confidence: low that it matters above 400 nm; listed because it is the one acknowledged instrument artifact inside the SDP range with no fix in V3.x.
- Version sensitivity (section 4).
  - Evidence: V3.2 changed the gas table, the vicarious gains and the BRDF correction.
  - Size: unmeasured.
  - Confidence: high that V3_1 and V3_2 outputs must not be mixed or compared without a rerun. Not a current problem: all 103 local 8-day files are V3_2. It will recur with the next reprocessing.

Checked and judged not to matter, with the reason:

- OCI's 5 nm resolution and 2.5 nm sampling as such. Kramer's own test shows little loss at 5 nm, Vandermeulen puts the useful resolution at 5 to 7 nm, and the training spectra were smoothed with a 5 nm mean.
- Different observation sets at different wavelengths in a composite. Masks are per pixel and no unmasked pixel has partial fill, so every band of a bin averages the same spectra.
- Random, band-independent instrument noise from 400 to 588 nm. About 0.15 percent of Rrs per L2 pixel near 442 nm; OCI exceeds its signal-to-noise requirement by 22 to 80 percent.
- Aerosol-correction error. It is large in Rrs (most of `Rrs_unc`) but nearly identical in neighbouring bands (correlation 0.97 to 0.99), so it mostly cancels in a second derivative, as Kramer expected. It still enters through the semi-analytical fit.
- The V3.1 cross-track BRDF error and the polarization geometry error. Fixed in V3.2 and smooth in wavelength.
- Ghosting stripes at 650 to 900 nm and the red vicarious over-calibration of V3.0. Fixed in V3.1 and inherited by V3.2.
- Scan-edge red elevation and cloud straylight. Masked out of L3 since V2 (view zenith above 60 degrees, and 1 by 2 pixels around bright targets).
- Negative Rrs in clear water. Zero from 490 to 588 nm, 0.3 percent at 442 nm, about 1 percent at 665 to 687 nm in one granule; the blue negatives come from shelf water. Rare enough that they are a flagging matter, not a bias, offshore.
- UV Rrs below 400 nm being unvalidated. Outside the 400 to 700 nm range SDP uses, except that the 5 nm smoothing and the derivative at 400 to 402 nm touch the edge.
- NO2 and ozone residuals offshore. Broad compared with the derivative window, corrected since V3.0, and no report of residuals; could matter close to the coast.
- The L1B temporal-gain date offset (V3.0.2). At most 0.3 percent above 400 nm and smooth in wavelength.
- Coccolithophore masking removed in V3.2 L3 AOP. Not an Rrs quality problem; it only admits a water type outside the training set on the shelf and slope in late spring, which the regional reviewer may want to mask.

### Gaps
- None of the "real issues" has a measured effect in pigment units. The quickest tests, all on data already local: (1) rerun SDP with the 588 to 613 nm derivative points replaced by the fitted model and with the 680 to 700 nm points zeroed, and compare; (2) compute the spread of SDP pigments among L2 pixels inside one 4 km bin on a clear day; (3) compare SDP on the 8-day mean spectrum with the unclipped mean of daily L2 SDP for the same bins.
- The second-derivative statistics here come from one V3.2 granule on 2025-04-04. Repeat on a humid summer granule before relying on the red-end numbers.
