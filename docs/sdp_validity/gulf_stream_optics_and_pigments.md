## 1. Optics: CDOM and detrital slopes, backscatter slopes, CDOM against chlorophyll, and tests of GSM, GIOP, QAA in the region

### Takeaway
The slopes SDP assumes are not the GSM01 constants. Its CDOM-plus-detritus slope (0.0148 to 0.0171 nm-1) sits inside the range measured on NAAMES, but its backscatter exponent goes to about 2.0 in clear Sargasso water while the measured values in the same region are about -0.4 to 1.2, lowest in Sargasso water. The published regional test (NAAMES) found that GSM gets chlorophyll and bbp(443) well, gets CDM poorly, and that no inversion recovers either slope. A wrong smooth slope does not leave a purely smooth residual, because the fit trades it against chlorophyll and because the residual is divided by pure-water absorption, which has sharp shoulders near 515, 600 and 660 nm.

### Cited Findings
What SDP actually assumes
- adg slope is `-(0.01447 + 0.00033 * Rrs490/Rrs555)`, so 0.0148 nm-1 at a ratio of 1 and 0.0171 nm-1 at a ratio of 8. [local] Source: `/Users/jerry/school/research/eddy-tracking/src/eddy_tracking/packages/sdp/physics.py` line 284; the paper's Eq. 5 prints the same coefficients with the sign written differently, [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879), local PDF `/Users/jerry/Zotero/storage/3S3D8JQG/`.
- bbp exponent is `2.0 * (1 - 1.2 * exp(-0.9 * rrs440/rrs555))`, the QAA form the paper attributes to Lee et al. (2002). It gives 1.02 at a ratio of 1, 1.60 at 2, 1.84 at 3, 1.97 at 5. [local, arithmetic inferred] Source: same file, line 300.
- Pure-water absorption is one fixed table (Mason et al. 2016) with no temperature or salinity term. Only pure-water backscatter uses temperature and salinity (Zhang et al. 2009). The paper used World Ocean Atlas climatology for those. [local] Source: same file lines 269 to 295; [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) section 2.3.
- The fit is an unconstrained simplex search on three numbers (chlorophyll, adg(443), bbp(443)) from the start point 0.15, 0.01, 0.0029. [local] Source: same file lines 196 to 233.
- The phytoplankton absorption shape is aph = A * Tchla^B with A and B fitted to NOMAD at 18 wavelengths and splined to 1 nm. [local] Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) Eq. 3.
- The paper states that the residual "to a large extent" represents the difference between the Tchla to accessory pigment relation in NOMAD and in the sample, and that repeating the regression on derivatives of the measured Rrs (no GSM step) gave "comparable results" (their Figs. S6 to S8, Table S1). [local] Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) sections 4 and 4.5.

### Inferences
- The band ratio Rrs490/Rrs555 is about 4 to 5 in Sargasso water and 1.5 to 2.5 in slope water (typical values, recalled), so SDP uses S of about 0.016 and 0.015 nm-1. That is 0.004 to 0.005 below GSM01 and inside the NAAMES acdm range of 0.013 to 0.021. The CDOM slope is therefore not the main mismatch, except in strongly bleached summer surface water where S is expected to be steeper (direction verified by Vodacek, magnitude for the Sargasso side not found).
- The bbp exponent is the larger mismatch. On the Sargasso side SDP assumes about 2.0 where NAAMES measured about 0 to 1. With the amplitude pinned in the blue, modeled bbp is 30 to 45% too low by 650 nm, since (443/650)^1 is 0.68 and (443/650)^1.5 is 0.56. In clear water that is roughly 10 to 20% of total backscatter in the orange and red, because pure-water backscatter is of the same order as bbp there.
- A smooth error in adg or bbp does not give a smooth residual in Rrs, for two reasons. First, the three-parameter fit absorbs part of it into chlorophyll, and the chlorophyll term carries the fixed NOMAD aph shape, so the residual gains an aph-shaped component of either sign with features near 440 and 675 nm. Second, Rrs goes as bb/(a + bb), and above 550 nm a is almost all pure water, so any fractional error in bb is multiplied by 1/aw, which drops by a factor of about 2.5 between 580 and 605 nm and has further shoulders near 515 and 660 nm. The second derivative of that product is not small at those wavelengths.
- Order of magnitude at Tchla 0.05 mg m-3: a 10 to 20% backscatter misfit at 580 to 650 nm is about 1.5e-5 to 1e-4 sr-1 with the water-shoulder shape, against accessory-pigment features in the blue of very roughly 2e-4 to 5e-4 sr-1 and a chlorophyll a red-peak absorption feature of only about 4e-7 sr-1. So in the orange and red the second derivative at low chlorophyll is controlled by water structure, backscatter misfit, Raman and fluorescence, not by pigment absorption. Confidence in the numbers: low, they are back-of-envelope; confidence in the direction: moderate.
- This matters less than it sounds if the training samples carry the same misfit, because the regression was fitted through it. It becomes a regional problem only where the misfit differs systematically from the training set, which is why the bbp slope (lowest in Sargasso water, highest in winter mixing) deserves a direct check.
- The exponential slope itself contributes little to a second derivative. For exp(-S * lambda) the second derivative is S^2 times the function, about 2.6e-4 nm-2, while a pigment band of 10 to 20 nm standard deviation gives 2.5e-3 to 1e-2 nm-2 times its amplitude. A smooth adg misfit must be 10 to 40 times a pigment feature in amplitude to matter equally.

## 2. Communities: HPLC studies across the front and whether regional pigment covariation matches the global one

### Takeaway
The regional HPLC record is good on the Sargasso side (BATS, monthly since 1988) and on the shelf, thin in slope water, and close to absent inside rings since the 1980s. The relation between cyanobacterial pigments and chlorophyll changes sign depending on whether the variation is spatial (across the front, Zea/Tchla falls as Tchla rises) or temporal at a Sargasso site (winter mixing raises everything together). A global regression mostly learns the spatial relation. Published cross-validation by spatial block found the two blocks off the North American east coast among the worst for most diagnostic pigments.

### Cited Findings

Pigments include Chl c3, Chl c2, peridinin, ButFuco, Fuco, HexFuco, prasinoxanthin, diadinoxanthin, alloxanthin, diatoxanthin, lutein, zeaxanthin, Chl b, Chl a. [abstract] Source: [BCO-DMO dataset 893521](https://www.bco-dmo.org/dataset/893521).
- In the BATS ERDDAP table chlorophyll a is not split into monovinyl and divinyl, and zeaxanthin appears both alone and summed with lutein. [local] Source: project memory `project_hplc_pigment_covariation_check.md`.
- NAAMES HPLC (229 samples, four cruises): five pigment communities. Zea, DVchla and DVchlb form a cyanobacteria cluster "strongly correlated to each other and separate from all other pigments". HexFuco, ButFuco and Chl c3 form the haptophyte cluster. Fuco and Chl c1+c2 form the diatom cluster. Perid stands alone. [web] Source: [Kramer, Siegel and Graff 2020, Frontiers in Marine Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00215/full).

### Inferences
- No training sample is from this region in autumn or winter, and none from this region is below about 0.05 mg m-3. What the regression knows about very low chlorophyll comes from the summer Mediterranean and the South Pacific gyre. The Sargasso side from September to January is outside the training envelope in season, in mixing regime, and partly in chlorophyll.
- The sign change of the Zea to Tchla relation is the most likely ecological reason for the negative SDP against HPLC zeaxanthin correlation. The method paper says cyanobacterial pigments are tied to the residual less directly than other groups and tend to run inverse to Tchla. A model that mostly encodes "less chlorophyll signal, more zeaxanthin" will be right across the front in summer and wrong in time at a Sargasso site, where mixing raises Synechococcus and chlorophyll together.
- HexFuco and Zea ratios peaking together on NAAMES 3 (late summer, lowest Tchla) is a real case of positive covariation of the two ratios, but it is between cruises, driven by the shared low denominator. It does not support r 0.94 within one region and season.
- Rings add a time dimension the training set lacks: ring 82B and the cold-core ring both show communities that are neither parent water after some months. An eddy-age signal in SDP pigments cannot be validated against any HPLC taken inside a ring in the PACE era that I could find.

## 3. Low chlorophyll: is the phytoplankton absorption signal in Rrs large enough on the Sargasso side

### Takeaway
At 0.03 to 0.1 mg m-3 the answer from the literature is that even the amplitude of phytoplankton absorption is marginal in satellite Rrs, before any accessory pigment is asked for. The method paper's own cross-validated error for Tchla is larger in absolute terms than the Sargasso chlorophyll itself. This is the strongest single reason for the clipped Tchla and the impossible DVchla/Tchla ratios.

### Cited Findings
- With OCI-like hyperspectral data, "one may retrieve four parameters for IOPs ... and possibly a fifth"; "we may only be able to extract a single parameter describing phytoplankton, e.g., the a_ph amplitude at about 440 nm"; "only about 30% of the L23 dataset favors the model with phytoplankton", the failures being mainly at lower chlorophyll. [web] Source: [Prochaska and Frouin 2025, Biogeosciences 22: 4705](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/22/4705/2025/).
- A low-chlorophyll PACE example in that paper: retrieved aph(440) 0.0084 +/- 0.0033 m-1 against a true 0.0073 m-1; the 99% interval "includes vanishingly small values", so phytoplankton is not detected at 3 sigma. For aph(440) above 0.01 m-1 the bias is 15% and the mean absolute error 40%, above the 35% PACE requirement. [web] Source: same.
- "Achieving S/N = 20 is challenging, even at blue wavelengths in open-ocean Case 1 waters", and may be impossible above 600 nm. The authors also say a machine-learning model "cannot learn how to distinguish" absorption from backscatter changes in the presence of noise, and cite Kramer et al. (2022) among such models. [web] Source: same.
- Method paper, Table 2: Tchla cross-validated R2 0.72 with mean absolute deviation equal to 0.498 of the mean retrieved concentration, statistics "assessed on a linear scale". Zea 0.472, DVchla 0.594, HexFuco 0.692, Fuco 0.844. [local] Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879).
- Method paper on noise: the derivative "magnifies narrow spectral features, including measurement noise and error"; "even small variations on short (5 to 10 nm) spectral scales are ultimately important"; in situ noise is much lower than satellite noise, so "spatiotemporal aggregation of remotely-sensed Rrs will likely be required". [local] Source: same, section 4.1.
- PACE OCI against AERONET-OC, 895 matchups, March 2024 to September 2025: median symmetric accuracy about 22.6% overall, 17.2% on average from 400 to 560 nm in clear water. [abstract] Source: [Mississippi State repository listing](https://scholarsjunction.msstate.edu/works_publications/203).
- NASA's PACE matchup page lists unbiased RMSE of 0.00173 sr-1 at 400 nm and 0.00133 sr-1 at 412 nm. [abstract] Source: [PACE data matchups](https://pace.oceansciences.org/pace_data_matchups.htm).
- Locally measured: at BATS, SDP Tchla against HPLC has log-space r of about -0.06 to -0.15 and reads high by about 1.7 times; SDP "carries no rank information at BATS or SO-PACE". [local] Source: project memory `project_sdp_bats_validation.md`, `project_sdp_pvst_validation.md`.

### Inferences
- Mean Tchla of the 145 training samples, weighted from Table 1, is about 0.31 mg m-3, so the cross-validated absolute error for Tchla is about 0.15 mg m-3. That is 1.5 to 5 times the Sargasso-side concentration of 0.03 to 0.1. A linear regression with that error, evaluated at the bottom of its range, will return negative values for a share of pixels. Clipping at zero is the expected behaviour, not a regional anomaly. Confidence: high on the arithmetic, moderate that this is the whole story.
- The same logic explains why Zea and DVchla "stay normal" while Tchla clips. Each pigment has its own regression. Zea has little dynamic range in training and R2 0.37, so its predictions sit near the training mean whatever the spectrum does. Tchla spans two decades, so its predictions move. A near-constant numerator over a denominator falling toward zero gives DVchla/Tchla above 1 and makes every pigment ratio covary with every other, which reproduces the r 0.94 between HexFuco/Tchla and Zea/Tchla. This matches the local finding that removing the Tchla dependence breaks most of that correlation.
- Bricaud-type relations put aph(440) at roughly 0.005 to 0.012 m-1 for 0.03 to 0.1 mg m-3 (recalled). That brackets the Prochaska and Frouin example of 0.0073 m-1, which was not detected at 3 sigma. Accessory-pigment features are a fraction of that amplitude.
- An 8-day 4 km composite helps random noise but not the spectrally correlated error from atmospheric correction, which is the larger term in the blue.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that states a chlorophyll detection floor for SDP or for any derivative-based pigment method on satellite data.
- I did not retrieve OCI random noise at L3 8-day 4 km as a function of wavelength. The colleague covering PACE data quality should own that number; the second derivative needs the spectrally uncorrelated part, not the total uncertainty.

## 4. Photoacclimation and packaging: does the Rrs to pigment relation shift with season or light history

### Takeaway
The mechanism is well established: zeaxanthin per cell is held roughly constant in Synechococcus and Prochlorococcus while chlorophyll per cell changes with light, so Zea/Tchla is a light-history index as much as a community index. Winter mixing below 80 m at 30 to 40 N therefore lowers Zea/Tchla without any change in who is there. I did not find a paper that measures how much this shifts an Rrs-based pigment retrieval in this region, so the size is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- In an oceanic Synechococcus acclimated across light levels, chlorophyll a, beta-carotene and phycoerythrin per cell varied strongly while "the zeaxanthin content remained approximately constant whatever the light level". [abstract] Source: [Six et al., Synechococcus WH8102](https://hal-ensta-paris.archives-ouvertes.fr/UPMC/hal-01663373).
- In Prochlorococcus, zeaxanthin is present "in stable amounts within the cells whatever the irradiance" and moderates how the absorption efficiency varies with light. [abstract] Source: [Bricaud et al. 1999](https://daniel-vaulot.fr/files/papers/Bricaud_et%20al_1999.pdf).
- In chemostat Synechococcus the zeaxanthin to chlorophyll a ratio decreased with increasing growth rate. [abstract] Source: [Marine Biology listing](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00392609).
- In the western Pacific the Zea to Chl a ratio was inversely related to chlorophyll and positively related to SST. [abstract] Source: search summary, [Gayana 2004 listing](https://doaj.org/article/aa50ad80dcc445b1a957627c9a0b3aac).
- The NAAMES pigment paper warns that if accessory-to-Tchla ratios shift with light, relations built on historical data relating pigment ratios to taxonomy will not describe the new relationships, and names plasticity with light, nutrients and strain as a complication. [web, paraphrase] Source: [Kramer, Siegel and Graff 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.00215/full).
- Sargasso mixing regime: winter mixing deeper than 80 m, summer and fall mixed layer about 50 m. [abstract] Source: [Treusch et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3280133).
- Pigment assemblage and packaging both shape absorption spectra, and over 99% of the variance in absorption spectra among phytoplankton is shared, which limits discrimination by shape (Garver et al. 1994, as summarized). [local] Source: Cetinić et al. 2024, local PDF `/Users/jerry/Zotero/storage/WETYFEG8/`.
- The SDP phytoplankton shape is a single-valued function of Tchla fitted to NOMAD; the paper says the residual reflects where the sample departs from the NOMAD relation between Tchla and accessory pigments. [local] Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) section 4.5.

### Inferences
- Photoacclimation enters SDP twice. It changes the true pigment ratios, which the regression can only follow if the training set saw the same light regime. It also changes the chlorophyll-specific absorption shape, so the residual against the fixed NOMAD shape shifts with season even at constant community. With no autumn or winter samples from this region in training, both shifts are unlearned.
- Direction for September to January on the Sargasso side: deepening mixing lowers mean light, cells raise chlorophyll per cell, Zea/Tchla falls and packaging rises slightly. HPLC zeaxanthin concentration can stay flat or fall while chlorophyll rises. A model keyed to the summer or spatial relation would then move zeaxanthin the wrong way, which is consistent with the negative matchup correlation. Confidence: moderate on direction, low on size.
- Zeaxanthin absorbs between about 450 and 500 nm and does not pass energy to photosynthesis (recalled), so its Rrs feature overlaps the carotenoid shoulder of every other group. Its separability in the second derivative is weaker than that of chlorophyll b or c, which fits its R2 of 0.37 in training.

### Gaps
- No regional study was found that quantifies the seasonal change of chlorophyll-specific absorption at BATS or in slope water. Bidigare et al. (1989, Journal of Marine Research 47: 323 to 341, western Sargasso Sea) is in the method paper's reference list and was not opened.
- No paper was found that tests a hyperspectral pigment algorithm for a seasonal bias. This looks untested rather than tested and passed.

## 5. Regional confounders of the Rrs spectrum itself

### Takeaway
For open-ocean eddies the confounders that can plausibly reach the second derivative are few: low signal, the water-absorption shoulders acting on smooth misfits (backscatter slope, Raman, water temperature), chlorophyll fluorescence, and summer Sargassum. Whitecaps, glint, salinity, coccolithophores and Trichodesmium are spectrally smooth or rare here and can be set aside. The atmospheric terms bias the GSM step and the band ratios that set the slopes, not the narrow features, unless trace-gas correction leaves residuals, which I could not check.

### Cited Findings
- Sargassum: satellite climatology for 2002 to 2008 puts the Atlantic centre of Sargassum near 37 N, 67 W at the start of the cycle, moving east until October and southwest until February; high abundance sits in the Gulf Stream extension in late summer and early fall and in the southern Sargasso Sea in winter and early spring. MODIS detection limit is about 0.2% of a pixel. [abstract] Sources: [Gower and King, core.ac.uk copy](https://files.core.ac.uk/download/pdf/287828.pdf); [Brooks et al. 2018](https://science.whoi.edu/users/mcgillic/Sargassum/pdfs/Brooks_2018_MEPS_m599p001.pdf); [Wang dissertation](https://science.whoi.edu/users/mcgillic/Sargassum/pdfs/Wang-dissertation-Sargassum-final.pdf).
- Whitecaps: reflectance averages about 40% in the visible and is treated as spectrally flat from 400 to 700 nm; its structure (troughs at 980 and 1200 nm) is in the near and shortwave infrared; the wind-speed correction used operationally has large scatter. [abstract] Source: [Dierssen 2019, Frontiers in Earth Science](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2019.00014/text).
- Aerosols: in the western Sargasso Sea dust depresses and sulfate raises SeaWiFS blue water-leaving radiance, both inflating chlorophyll; sulfate is "nearly constant" with co-located pollutant aerosol and judged the more serious problem. [abstract] Source: [Schollaert et al. 2003](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/gsofacpubs/362).
- The method paper expects atmospheric-correction error on broad spectral scales and argues the derivative "will negate many of these issues". [local] Source: [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) section 4.1.
- Raman scattering: ignoring it puts average errors of about 50% on bbp from semi-analytical inversions in clear ocean water. [abstract] Source: [Westberry, Boss and Lee 2013, Applied Optics 52: 5552](https://digitalcommons.library.umaine.edu/sms_facpub/163).
- Fluorescence: the 683 nm peak is visible in NAAMES Rrs, and the method paper finds some of the strongest correlations between residual derivatives and pigments "in the red, where chlorophyll both absorbs and fluoresces". [web, local] Sources: [Allen et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2020.567007/full); [Kramer et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2021.112879) section 3.3.
- Pure-water temperature dependence: in the visible the dependence is below 0.001 m-1 per degree C except near 610 nm; hyperspectral measurements put the peaks at 604, 662 and 740 nm. [abstract] Sources: [Pegau et al. 1997, listing](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/concern/defaults/2f75r934v); [Sullivan et al. 2006](https://misclab.umeoce.maine.edu/ftp/classes/OO2011/incoming/Sullivanetal2006.pdf); coefficients tabulated in [Röttgers et al. 2014](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/50986/).
- Coccolithophores: Northern Hemisphere blooms start in February to March near 30 N and last 6 to 7 months, later and shorter poleward; they often co-occur with other phytoplankton in the open ocean. [abstract] Source: [Hopkins et al. 2015](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/374858/1/gbc20248.pdf).
- Trichodesmium: satellite detection needs surface mats larger than a pixel, which give a near-infrared red edge. [abstract] Source: [Biogeosciences 15: 5249](https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/15/5249/2018/).
- Flags in local PACE matchups: per 25-pixel box, cloud or ice 13.5 pixels, high sensor zenith 8.3, stray light 2.9, high glint 0.4, high solar zenith 0.0. [local] Source: project memory `project_sdp_pvst_validation.md`.

### Inferences
Each item below says whether it applies to open-ocean eddies here, when, and whether it reaches the second derivative between 400 and 700 nm.
- Floating Sargassum. Applies July to October along the Gulf Stream extension and in convergences at eddy edges. It adds a land-plant red edge above 700 nm and brown-algal pigment absorption (fucoxanthin, chlorophyll c, chlorophyll a) to the pixel, and the raised near-infrared pushes the aerosol correction to remove too much blue. At 0.1% cover the visible change is of order 1e-5 sr-1 with pigment structure, small against blue pigment features but comparable to the orange and red ones; at 1% cover it is not small. It is structured, so the derivative does not remove it.
- Trichodesmium. Mostly south of this region and rarely in surface mats at 35 to 42 N (recalled). Does not apply to these eddies in any season that matters.
- Coccolithophores. Blooms are a shelf, Gulf of Maine and subpolar feature (recalled); in slope and Sargasso water detached coccoliths raise backscatter smoothly. Applies weakly in spring and summer on the slope side. Smooth, so it reaches the derivative only through the water-shoulder effect in question 1.
- Whitecaps and bubbles. Apply November to March. Flat in the visible, so no direct second-derivative feature. They cost valid pixels and can bias the aerosol estimate, which is smooth.
- High solar zenith. PACE crosses near 13:00 local time, so the sun is about 59 to 60 degrees from zenith at 35 N and 64 to 65 degrees at 40 N at the December solstice (my arithmetic). The NASA high-solar-zenith flag is at 70 degrees (recalled), so these pixels are processed, close to the limit, from November to January. The effect is a smooth bias in blue Rrs and in the bidirectional correction, with lower signal-to-noise. It degrades the GSM step and the band ratios, not the narrow features.
- Absorbing aerosols, continental outflow, smoke and dust. Apply all year for pollution, summer for dust and smoke. Verified to bias blue Rrs and chlorophyll in exactly this water. Smooth in wavelength, so the derivative is largely immune, but the blue-to-green ratios that set SDP's two slopes are not. Unverified: whether nitrogen dioxide (structured absorption from 400 to 460 nm, high in northeast US outflow), ozone, or water-vapour correction leaves narrow residuals in OCI Rrs. If it does, those are fixed-wavelength features that vary with season and air mass, which is the worst case for a derivative method. This belongs to the PACE data-quality review.
- Sun glint. Applies May to August. OCI tilts to avoid it and high-glint pixels are masked; local matchups lost 0.4 of 25 pixels. Residual glint is spectrally flat. Does not reach the derivative.
- Chlorophyll fluorescence near 683 nm. Applies everywhere, all year. It is a narrow feature, about 25 nm wide (recalled), so the second derivative keeps all of it, and the regression is known to use the red. Its yield per unit chlorophyll changes with light history and nutrient stress by a factor of about 2 to 3 (recalled), so the same pigment load gives a different red feature in winter than in summer. At 0.05 mg m-3 the feature is small against noise.
- Raman scattering. Applies most in the clearest water, so on the Sargasso side, all year. It is not in SDP's forward model. The verified effect is a large bbp bias. The Raman contribution is broad, but like the backscatter misfit it is divided by water absorption, so it adds to the water-shoulder structure near 580 to 610 nm. It scales with clarity, so it covaries with chlorophyll across the front.
- Temperature and salinity dependence of pure water. Applies across the front all year and most in winter, when slope water and Gulf Stream surface water differ by 10 to 15 degrees C. SDP's water absorption has no temperature term. At about 0.001 m-1 per degree C near 605 nm, a 10 degree difference changes absorption by about 0.01 m-1 against 0.24 to 0.26 m-1, about 4% of Rrs in a band 30 to 40 nm wide, roughly 1e-5 sr-1 in clear water. That is 20 to 40 times smaller than blue pigment features but larger than any pigment absorption feature in the orange and red at low chlorophyll, and it is perfectly correlated with SST, which is itself the strongest environmental predictor of pigments (Stock and Subramaniam). Salinity changes pure-water backscatter by a few percent over 32 to 36.5 and SDP already takes salinity, so it does not matter.

Ranked for open-ocean eddies, most likely to matter first. Evidence, size, confidence:
- 1. Signal too small on the Sargasso side, June to January. Evidence: Prochaska and Frouin (2025); method paper Table 2; local BATS matchups with no rank skill. Size: training error about 0.15 mg m-3 against 0.03 to 0.1 present. Confidence: high.
- 2. Regional and seasonal pigment covariation unlike the training set. Evidence: sign change of the Zea to Tchla slope between BATS and upwelling water (local); east-coast blocks worst in spatial cross-validation; no autumn or winter regional training samples. Size: sign errors for Zea, compressed range elsewhere. Confidence: high that it applies, moderate on how much each pigment suffers.
- 3. Photoacclimation of Zea/Tchla and of the absorption shape under winter mixing. Evidence: culture studies (abstracts); mixing depths. Size: not quantified for this region. Confidence: moderate.
- 4. Backscatter-slope misfit and Raman acting through the water-absorption shoulders. Evidence: SDP exponent near 2.0 against measured -0.4 to 1.2 (NAAMES); 50% bbp bias from Raman. Size: up to 10 to 20% of Rrs at 580 to 650 nm, shaped like 1/aw. Confidence: moderate on existence, low on whether training already absorbs it.
- 5. Atmospheric bias in the blue from absorbing aerosol and high solar zenith. Evidence: Schollaert et al. (2003) in this water. Size: enough to move SeaWiFS chlorophyll; effect on the derivative small unless trace-gas residuals exist. Confidence: moderate for the GSM step, low for the derivative.
- 6. Water-temperature feature near 605 nm and 662 nm. Evidence: laboratory coefficients; SDP has no temperature term. Size: about 4% of Rrs near 605 nm per 10 degrees C. Confidence: moderate that it exists in the residual, low that the regression weights it. Cheap to test.
- 7. Fluorescence yield changes. Size: a factor of 2 to 3 in yield, small in absolute Rrs at low chlorophyll. Confidence: low to moderate.
- 8. Sargassum in summer and early fall. Size: negligible below 0.1% cover, real near 1%. Confidence: low to moderate, patchy.
- 9. CDOM slope mismatch and seasonal bleaching. Size: SDP's S is inside the measured range; smooth. Confidence: moderate that it does not matter for the derivative, though it moves GSM chlorophyll.

Checked and judged not to matter for open-ocean eddies, with the reason:
- Whitecaps and bubbles: flat in the visible; they remove pixels, they do not add features.
- Sun glint: tilted sensor, masked, flat.
- Salinity dependence of water: a few percent in backscatter, already supplied to the model.
- Trichodesmium: wrong latitude for surface mats.
- Coccolithophore blooms: shelf and subpolar; open-water effect is smooth backscatter.
- GSM non-convergence: a turbid-coast failure; no BATS or SO-PACE box failed.
- The CDOM slope as a direct source of second-derivative error: an exponential contributes S^2, about 2.6e-4 nm-2, far below a pigment band.
- Inner-shelf turbidity and river CDOM: reach eddies only in shelf-water streamers around warm-core rings, where they come with slope-water communities and should be treated as real signal mixed with a different optical regime, not as a spectrum artifact.

### Gaps
- Trace-gas residuals in OCI Rrs (nitrogen dioxide, ozone, water vapour, oxygen B band near 687 nm) were not researched here.
- I did not verify the 70 degree solar-zenith threshold, the L3 default mask list, or whether a coccolithophore flag removes pixels from L3 Rrs. All three are recalled.
- No measurement of Sargassum cover per 4 km pixel north of 35 N was found, and I did not check 2024 to 2026 Sargassum conditions.
- The temperature at which the Mason et al. (2016) water absorption table was measured was not checked; the size of the temperature residual depends on it.
- The magnitude of the Raman fraction of Rrs by wavelength in Sargasso water was not retrieved (only the 50% bbp figure).

## 6. In situ datasets that pair HPLC with hyperspectral Rrs or with PACE overpasses since March 2024

The best regional check that exists is at BATS: hyperspectral Rrs from the PACE Sargasso Sea Team on the same cruises as the BATS and PVST plankton HPLC, which removes atmospheric correction from the test. ### Inferences
- Running SDP on PVST_PSST in situ Rrs and scoring against same-cruise HPLC separates two questions the satellite matchups cannot: whether SDP fails at BATS because of PACE Rrs, or because of the water. The cruise list spans August 2024 to May 2026, so it covers two autumn to winter seasons.
- The same in situ spectra give direct tests of items 4 and 6 in the ranked list: regress the second-derivative residual near 600 nm on SST at fixed chlorophyll, and refit with the bbp exponent set to 0.5 or 1.0 to see how much the residual and the pigments move.
